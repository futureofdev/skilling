# Your first lesson

A Skilling lesson is a conversation with a simple rhythm: **explain, question, revisit.**

1. **Explain.** The tutor introduces one small idea, usually with an example.
2. **Question.** It asks you to put the idea in your own words, and then it waits.
3. **Revisit.** If your answer shows a gap, it explains that part another way.

The question isn't a test you have to pass. It shows both of you what's clear and what needs
another look.

## You're in charge of the pace

You can interrupt at any point. Things that work well:

- "Can you slow down?"
- "Give me a different example."
- "I don't get the second part. Can you go back to it?"
- "Can we stop here for today?"

Your answers don't need to be polished. A rough answer in your own words tells the tutor much
more than repeating its words back.

## Exercises

Most lessons have a hands-on exercise. Some ask you to decide, predict or explain something.
Others have you try a command or change a file. Where it can, the tutor handles the mechanical
typing through your assistant's usual permission prompts, and you make the decisions that
matter.

## Quizzes

Every lesson ends with a short quiz: three questions, four options each. The tutor reads out
why the right answer is right, even when you got it. If you get one wrong, it offers to explain
the idea again, and it's your call whether to take it. A wrong answer never stops you finishing
the lesson.

## Stopping and coming back

Close your assistant whenever you like. Next time, open the same folder and type `/learn` (or
`$learn` in Codex). The tutor reads your saved progress and carries on from there. It doesn't
need the old conversation.
