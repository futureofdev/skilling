## The Concept

A useful tutoring conversation has a simple rhythm: **explain → question → revisit**.

First, the tutor explains a small piece of the subject with a concrete example. Then the
tutor asks a question and waits for your answer. The question is not a test of whether you
deserve to continue. It helps both of you see what is clear and what needs attention.

The revisit is where the conversation becomes personal. If your answer shows a gap, the
tutor can explain that part another way. If the explanation was too quick or too abstract,
you can ask for a slower pace or a different example. You remain in control of that choice.

Your answer does not need to sound polished. Putting an idea into your own words gives the
tutor more useful information than repeating its words back. The tutor can guide your
reflection, but should not write it for you.

## Key Terms

- **Explain**: Present a small idea clearly, usually with an example
- **Question**: Invite the learner to show what they understand in their own words
- **Revisit**: Return to an idea with a different explanation, example, or pace

## Hands-On Exercise

Choose an everyday subject you know something about: making tea, caring for a plant, planning
a journey, or anything else familiar to you.

Ask your tutor to explain one small part of it. Answer the tutor's question in your own words.
Then request either a different example or a change of pace, even if the first explanation
made sense. Say what the change helped you notice. Keep that reflection in your own words;
your tutor may ask a follow-up, but should not supply the reflection for you.

## Quick Quiz

1. What is the purpose of the question after an explanation?
   - a) To decide whether the learner is allowed to continue
   - b) To reveal what is clear and what may need another look
   - c) To make the learner repeat the tutor's exact words
   - d) To replace the explanation with a score

   **Answer:** b) To reveal what is clear and what may need another look — the answer gives
   the tutor evidence for choosing a useful revisit.

2. Why should a learner answer in their own words?
   - a) It gives the tutor useful evidence of the learner's understanding
   - b) It guarantees that every answer is correct
   - c) It lets the tutor skip the revisit
   - d) It makes the subject more formal

   **Answer:** a) It gives the tutor useful evidence of the learner's understanding — copied
   wording can hide a gap that an original explanation makes visible.

3. An explanation feels too abstract. What is a useful next move?
   - a) Pretend it made sense and move on
   - b) Ask the tutor to write your reflection for you
   - c) Ask for a different example or a slower pace
   - d) Abandon the subject completely

   **Answer:** c) Ask for a different example or a slower pace — changing the route can make
   the same idea clearer while keeping you in control of the conversation.
