## The Concept

Learning often happens in separate visits. A useful tutor remembers where you stopped, but
your progress should not depend on one conversation staying open forever.

Skilling keeps the durable parts in your learning workspace. Your course position records
where to continue. The `showcase` folder holds work you want to keep. When you return to the
workspace and ask to learn, the tutor can use that saved state instead of asking you to
reconstruct the whole conversation from memory.

The distinction matters: the progress record says *where you are*, while your own file says
*what matters to you*. A short goal, a takeaway in your words, and a concrete next action are
enough to turn a good conversation into something you can use later.

Because this is the declared final lesson of its phase and carries homework, completing it
saves your course position and makes that authored homework available. Completion does not
submit the homework. Submission happens separately, after you show the work to your tutor,
review feedback on each requirement, and explicitly confirm that you want to submit.

## Key Terms

- **Learning workspace**: The folder that keeps course content, progress, and learner work together
- **Progress**: The durable course position that lets learning continue after a break
- **Showcase**: The visible part of the workspace where the learner keeps their own work

## Hands-On Exercise

Create `showcase/welcome-skilling/goal.md` in the learning workspace. Use an editor yourself,
or dictate your exact wording and ask your tutor to save those words verbatim. Use these
labels:

- **Goal:** a subject or ability you want to develop
- **Takeaway:** something from this course that you can explain in your own words
- **Next action:** a small action you will take when you next learn

Make each entry specific enough that it will still be useful when you return. The ideas and
wording must remain yours whichever way the file is saved. Ask your tutor to verify that the
file exists at that exact workspace-relative path and that all entries contain meaningful
content. The tutor may ask you to make an entry clearer, but must not invent, rewrite, or
replace your words.

## Quick Quiz

1. What does saved course progress tell a returning tutor?
   - a) Which course position the learner should continue from
   - b) Everything the learner thought during the conversation
   - c) Which external account the learner used
   - d) That the learner must begin again

   **Answer:** a) Which course position the learner should continue from — durable position
   is enough to resume without depending on the old conversation.

2. Where should the learner keep the goal, takeaway, and next action from this course?
   - a) Only in the current conversation
   - b) In any location the tutor guesses
   - c) In `showcase/welcome-skilling/goal.md` inside the learning workspace
   - d) In a tutor-written summary that replaces the learner's words

   **Answer:** c) In `showcase/welcome-skilling/goal.md` inside the learning workspace — that
   visible course path keeps learner-authored work available when the learner returns.

3. What must happen before the tutor submits the homework?
   - a) The learner mentions that the work exists
   - b) The learner shows the work, reviews feedback, then confirms submission separately
   - c) The tutor decides that the learner probably agrees
   - d) The lesson completes, which also submits the homework

   **Answer:** b) The learner shows the work, reviews feedback, then confirms submission
   separately — sharing work or discussing feedback alone is not permission to submit, and
   lesson completion only makes this lesson's homework available.

## Homework Assignment

### Try the method

**Objective:** Apply explain, question, and revisit to something you want to learn.

- [ ] Choose a small topic and ask your tutor for a clear explanation
- [ ] Answer a question in your own words, then request a useful change of example or pace
- [ ] Add a brief review of what changed in your understanding to your saved goal file

**Submission:** Show your work to your tutor and review the feedback on each requirement. When that review is complete, explicitly confirm that you want to submit. This confirmation is separate from sharing the work or discussing the feedback.
